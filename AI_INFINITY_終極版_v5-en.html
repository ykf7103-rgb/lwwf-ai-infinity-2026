2027 Critical Skills

Five skills. Each one sits at the heart of AI-era education.

01 · ANALYTICAL

Analytical Thinking

Knowing what to ask. Knowing how to break a tangled problem apart. AI hands us answers — but only humans decide which questions are worth asking.

02 · CREATIVE

Creativity

AI scores eighty percent on autopilot. What separates the great from the merely good is human taste — a point of view AI cannot copy. Creativity becomes the rarest currency.

03 · AI LITERACY

AI & Big Data Literacy

Use AI. Question AI. Collaborate with AI. The skill isn't "chatting" — it's knowing when AI is wrong, and when its answers can be trusted.

04 · RESILIENCE

Resilience & Agility

AI never knows defeat. Students who can iterate through failure become the protagonists of their own stories. The ability to keep moving through uncertainty is the rarest trait of all.

05 · CURIOSITY

Curiosity + Self-Learning

Curiosity is the one human trait AI will never replace. It powers lifelong learning — the only real insurance against forty-four percent skill obsolescence.

❓ My child's English isn't strong enough — will English Elite be too hard?

Class A admissions emphasise learning interest rather than "current level". The June interview assesses adaptability. If students don't settle in, they can transfer to Class B/C between P.1 and P.2, with no pressure.

❓ Will Class B/C students get less English exposure?

No. The English subject itself is identical school-wide (same hours, same materials). Classes B/C simply have Math and Science taught in Chinese. All classes still have NET reading lessons and daily English activities.

❓ Why can't students join English Elite after P.2 → P.3?

Because from P.3 onwards, the accumulated knowledge in English-medium subjects is substantial (math word problems, lab reports). Joining mid-stream creates too much pressure. Mid-year entrants are handled case-by-case.

❓ Will teaching Math in English affect Chinese math concepts?

No. The Chinese and English versions of the worksheets have identical content (see the photo on the left); teachers switch between languages while teaching. Students master math concepts in both languages.

ASSESSMENT REFORM

Why LWWF Dropped Traditional Dictation

Moving from "memorisation testing" to "application testing" — answering what the AI era actually demands. LWWF has, since the 2024-25 academic year, gradually rolled out the no-dictation policy; Chinese and English no longer use traditional dictation as a regular assessment.

📌

Not "no more learning characters"

It's about "not using rote dictation to assess students."

Students still learn characters, know vocabulary, and use it — we just stop using rote dictation tests to measure progress.

2024
Launch Year
Chinese + English
Subjects Removed
Pillar 1

🔄 Free Up Learning Space · Go Deeper

Time previously spent on dictation prep is redirected to reading, writing, discussion, and speaking — building deeper language ability. The Hong Kong EDB endorsed this in 2025.

Pillar 2

💪 Lower Pressure · Protect Learning Interest

Traditional dictation breeds resistance to Chinese and English — multiple weekly tests, low scores punished. Without it, students keep loving the language.

Pillar 3

🤖 The AI Era · Rethinking Assessment

When AI can autocomplete spelling and transcribe speech, the value of "remembering how to write a character" collapses — what matters is application, judgement, and creation.

Pillar 4

📊 Multi-Track Assessment · Reflects Real Ability

We use reading, writing, speaking, class performance, project work — diverse methods aligned with real language use. Paired with the Three-Track Assessment.

THREE-TRACK ASSESSMENT

Three Tracks, Full Picture

Not abandoning assessment — moving from paper-and-pen alone to three parallel tracks. This closes the AI-ghostwriting blind spot and truly reflects ability.

🔬 Performance Assessment in Action

Performance + Oral Defence — students build and explain it themselves; AI ghostwriting is impossible

📝
Track 1 · Knowledge Base

Paper & Pen

Traditional tests and exams stay — covering core knowledge, computation, and writing.

Best for: core concepts · foundational skills · 30-40% weight

📂
Track 2 · Learning Process

Process Portfolio

Collects a term of work, reflections, drafts and self/peer review — capturing process, not just result.

Best for: creative work · writing · PBL · 30-35% weight

Performance + Oral Defence

Students demonstrate and explain their own work — our strongest defence against AI ghostwriting. Teachers probe design decisions on the spot.

INVITATIONAL EDUCATION

Five Elements Behind Our Talent Programme

Our Talent Programme is built on the international Invitational Education framework — with Care · Optimism · Respect · Trust · Intentionality — five elements that awaken every student's unique gifts.

⭐ 5 ELEMENTS · Student-Centred

The 5P Framework: People · Programs · Policies · Places · Processes

💜 CARE

Every student deserves to be seen, heard, and understood — teachers honour individuality and build psychological safety.

☀️ OPTIMISM

We believe every student has unlimited potential — failure is the next step toward success. Hope, always.

🤝 RESPECT

We respect each student's choices, interests, and pace — never forcing everyone to master the same skill at the same time.

🌟 TRUST

Teachers trust students with self-growth — letting go so they can explore and decide for themselves.

🎯 INTENTIONALITY

Every environment, activity, and assessment is intentionally designed — never random, always systematically inviting each student's potential.

Education isn't filling a bucket — it's lighting a fire. The Talent Programme helps every student find their spark.
— Dean Siu, Curriculum Coordinator
